# Editorial Voice

This skill enforces the editorial tone established by the visual references in `.claude/screenshots/`. Every word on the website should sound like it could appear in *Vogue Weddings*, an Aman Resorts press release, or an *Architectural Digest* feature.

## When to use this skill

- Writing hero headlines, section intros, or pull quotes
- Drafting any Sanity content
- Writing UI microcopy (button labels, form fields, error messages, empty states)
- Reviewing AI-drafted or contributor-drafted copy
- Translating copy (the voice must survive translation, not just the meaning)
- Naming things (page sections, CTAs, navigation items)

## The voice in one sentence

**Quietly authoritative, editorially restrained, emotionally precise — never commercial, never hurried, never decorative.**

## The five voice principles

### 1. Specificity over generality

❌ "We plan beautiful weddings around the world."
✅ "Fourteen years between Punta Cana and the Amalfi Coast. Four hundred weddings. One office."

❌ "We work with the best vendors."
✅ "Three florists in Punta Cana. Two in Cap Cana. We know which one will say no on a Tuesday in February."

The luxury reader respects expertise expressed through specificity. Generic copy reads as inexperienced.

### 2. Italic carries the headline

Headlines almost always use the structure: **[normal] [italic key word] [normal]**.

✅ "The art *of the destination* wedding."
✅ "A wedding *in Indian* tradition."
✅ "Houses we *have worked*, more than once."

The italic word is the emotional pivot — the verb of the headline. Pick it carefully.

### 3. Editorial fragments, not marketing sentences

The voice borrows from print journalism. Sentence fragments are welcome. Em-dashes carry weight. Lists are typeset, not bulleted.

✅ "Five days. Two hundred guests. One prayer."
✅ "Tradition, held by people who have lived inside it."
✅ "What we know — honestly."

❌ "We offer a comprehensive five-day planning service that includes..."
❌ "Discover our beautiful selection of luxury wedding venues."

### 4. No commercial language

Forbidden in any context: "amazing," "stunning," "perfect," "unforgettable," "magical," "incredible," "elevate," "luxurious" (the brand IS luxury — saying so is gauche), "exclusive," "premium," "world-class," "best-in-class."

The luxury is in the restraint. If a piece of copy could appear on a travel agency homepage, rewrite it.

Replacements:
- "Stunning venue" → "A pavilion in the palms" / "A villa above the cove" / "A garden between two olive trees"
- "Unforgettable" → say what specifically will be remembered
- "Magical" → describe the actual mechanic ("the moment the lanterns lifted")
- "Luxurious" → omit; let the details speak
- "Exclusive" → "Atelier," "considered," "small-list," or describe the cap ("forty couples a year")

### 5. Cultural fluency is the brand promise

When describing any cultural tradition:
- Use the **authentic name** (mandap, ketubah, jirga, mayyim chayim — not "wedding canopy" etc.)
- Never reduce a tradition to its visual elements
- Never use "exotic," "ethnic," "colorful" to describe cultural practices
- Hold sacred elements with gravity — they are not aesthetic choices

✅ "The mandap is not a decoration. It is a structure with four pillars, each representing one parent. The couple stands beneath it because the parents now hold the marriage."
❌ "A beautiful traditional mandap setup with stunning Indian decor."

## Length conventions

| Format | Target |
|---|---|
| Hero headline | 4-9 words (often italic structure) |
| Section eyebrow | 3-6 words, uppercase, tracked |
| Section heading (H2) | 4-10 words |
| Intro paragraph | 60-120 words |
| Body paragraph | 50-100 words |
| Pull quote | 12-25 words |
| Card titles | 2-5 words |
| Card body | 20-40 words |
| Button label | 2-5 words (uppercase or sentence-case) |
| Form field label | 1-3 words |
| Form helper text | 5-12 words |
| FAQ question | 8-15 words |
| FAQ answer | 40-120 words |
| Meta description | 140-160 chars |

## Naming conventions for sections

Section headers (the H2s on every page) should feel editorial, not navigational. Look at the screenshots:

- "An atelier of considered destinations"
- "Tradition, held by people who have lived inside it"
- "The houses of Punta Cana"
- "Houses we have worked, more than once"
- "Where the camera goes"
- "What we know honestly"
- "What's changed about Punta Cana weddings"
- "From the journal"
- "Begin your wedding journey"

Generic patterns to avoid:
- ❌ "Our Services"
- ❌ "Featured Venues"
- ❌ "About Us"
- ❌ "Why Choose Us"
- ❌ "Testimonials"

## CTAs — Grecia's specific palette

The brief identified these CTA variants. Use them; do not invent new ones lightly.

- *Request a Private Consultation*
- *Begin Your Wedding Journey*
- *Curate My Wedding Experience*
- *Plan Your Destination Wedding*
- *Speak With Grecia Mejia*

Pick based on context — the home page uses one, the contact form trigger uses another. Never use "Get Started," "Learn More," "Sign Up," "Book Now," or "Contact Us."

## Eyebrow labels (uppercase tracked text)

These small uppercase labels appear throughout the design. They are *categorical*, not promotional:

✅ "Plate 03"
✅ "An atelier"
✅ "From the journal"
✅ "In residence"
✅ "By region"
✅ "Considered honestly"

❌ "NEW!"
❌ "LIMITED TIME"
❌ "FEATURED"
❌ "POPULAR"

## Microcopy patterns

### Empty states

❌ "No results found. Try a different search."
✅ "Nothing yet in this register. The list is curated, not exhaustive."

### Form validation

❌ "Email is required."
✅ "We need your email to write back."

### Loading states

The luxury voice doesn't apologize. A simple ellipsis or a serif "—" is more in-keeping than "Loading, please wait..."

### 404 page

❌ "404 — Page not found."
✅ "Not on this list. / We have either retired this page, or you've arrived a little early. / Return to the atelier →"

## Translation considerations

The voice MUST survive translation. When working in non-English locales:

- **Use the locale's natural elegant register.** Italian uses indirect constructions. French embraces nominalization. Spanish prefers vivid concrete imagery. German is structurally precise.
- **Italic-key-word pattern translates well** to all five languages — preserve it
- **Cultural references shift per locale** — a French page might mention Provence first; an Italian page might mention Capri
- **Honorifics** — French formal "vous"; German formal "Sie"; Spanish "usted" (or "tú" depending on regional convention — use "tú" for warmth, "usted" for formal). Decide per market and apply consistently.
- **Never translate proper nouns** (Grecia Mejia, Jellyfish Restaurant, Eden Roc Cap Cana)

## Voice anti-patterns

Patterns that appear in lesser wedding sites and must NEVER appear here:

- ❌ "Say 'I do' to..." (any sentence that begins this way)
- ❌ "Make your dream wedding a reality"
- ❌ "Your love story deserves..."
- ❌ "The wedding of your dreams"
- ❌ "Tying the knot"
- ❌ "Walking down the aisle" used metaphorically
- ❌ Heavy use of "magical," "fairytale," "romantic" as descriptors
- ❌ Wedding industry jargon ("destination I do," "bling," "the big day")
- ❌ Exclamation marks in editorial copy
- ❌ Emoji anywhere
- ❌ All-caps shouting
- ❌ Smiley faces, hearts, or sparkles as decoration

## Voice checklist for any copy review

Before approving any copy, verify:

- [ ] No banned commercial words ("amazing," "stunning," etc.)
- [ ] No exclamation marks in editorial copy
- [ ] Italic key-word pattern used in at least the primary headlines
- [ ] At least one specific, concrete detail (number, name, place) per paragraph
- [ ] Cultural terminology used authentically (where applicable)
- [ ] Reads like print journalism, not web marketing
- [ ] Voice is consistent across the page (no shifts between elevated and casual)
- [ ] CTAs use the approved palette
- [ ] Length is within targets above
- [ ] Eyebrows are categorical, not promotional
- [ ] In translation: voice survives, doesn't sound like Google Translate
